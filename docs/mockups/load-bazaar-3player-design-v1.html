1 · What the market does, and what of it applies here the R&D, briefly

Ola / Uber (passenger)

  • Algorithmic price, no negotiation. The rider is quoted; the driver accepts or does not.
  • Live tracking share link is the trust device — a stranger opens it, sees the car move, and needs no account.
  • Applies here: the share link. Does not: the fixed price — your customers negotiate, and a bid board is the whole point.

Uber Freight / Convoy (broker)

  • The shipper pays X, the carrier is paid Y, and the broker keeps the spread. Neither side sees the other's number.
  • The desk's job is the spread, not the match — matching is easy, protecting the margin is not.
  • Applies here almost exactly. This is the model your directive describes.

Vahak / BlackBuck (Indian load boards)

  • Discovery-first: a big open board, phone numbers exchanged, the platform steps out of the deal.
  • Margin is a listing fee or a payments cut, not a spread.
  • Does not apply: stepping out of the deal is what you are trying to avoid. You carry the customer relationship and the risk.

The conclusion that shapes everything below

You are a broker who also owns trucks, not a listing site. So the engine has to protect two numbers that must never meet: what the customer agreed to pay, and what the partner agreed to accept. Every screen below exists to keep those apart and to make the difference between them — the margin — a thing the office decides on purpose rather than discovers afterwards.

Why the desk looks like this

  • The margin is shown per bid, not once. The desk's real question is "which bid, at what spread" — computing it in their head for each row is how a −₹1,500 deal gets accepted at 7 p.m.
  • A negative margin is shown in red, not hidden. Sometimes you take it to keep a customer. That must be a decision, not an accident.
  • Both numbers stay editable until the lock. A counter-offer to either side is the normal case, and a desk that can only accept what was typed is a desk people work around on WhatsApp.
  • Partner history is on the row — "4 trips done" vs "new partner" is most of why a desk picks the second-cheapest bid.
  • Lock is one irreversible moment. It writes the margin, generates the LR and posts the ledger together, so those three can never disagree.
4 · Maps what we already have, and the one upgrade

Places Autocomplete — already built

src/lib/PlaceInput.tsx does this today, with session tokens so a lookup is billed once instead of per keystroke. It degrades to free text on purpose — half your lanes are depot names Google has never heard of (“BONGAIGAON RC OFFICE (7R01)”), and a box that refuses them is a box the office cannot use.

Work: wire it into the load form. Nothing to invent.

Distance — already built, tolls — upgrade

server/lib/googleMaps.js gives distance, duration and a polyline, cached per lane in maps_cache. It calls the legacy Directions API, which returns no tolls. Google's Routes API v2 does.

But we have something better for your lanes: 3,883 real FASTag transactions in toll_transactions, joined to trips. Averaged per lane that is what the toll actually was, not an estimate.

The toll rule I propose

  1. Lane run 2+ times before → our own FASTag average. Label: “from 2 past runs”.
  2. Otherwise → Google Routes API estimate. Label: “Google estimate”.
  3. Neither → blank, never a guess.

The label is not decoration. A desk that cannot tell a measured number from an estimated one will treat both as fact.

5 · The customer's tracking link a stranger with a URL, and nothing else

Today /api/v1/share/:token is the only public prefix, and it serves exactly one stored file. A live tracking page is a different animal, so it gets its own narrow door rather than widening that one:

  • A token minted at deal-lock, expiring with the trip, revocable, and counted on every open — the same discipline the file door already keeps.
  • It shows: the lane, the truck number, the last known position with how old it is, and the delivery status. The dot animates along the route polyline we already cache.
  • It never shows: the partner's name or rate, the customer's rate, the margin, the driver's phone. A link forwarded to a WhatsApp group must not leak the deal.
  • The honest limit: a market truck has no GPS with us. Position comes from the partner's driver app when they use it, and the page says “no live position — last seen at 11:07” rather than drawing a dot we do not have.
